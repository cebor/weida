### B-044 — Decision 0011: how server-initiated traffic addresses a connection
kind: research | size: 45 | status: done a456f72 | needs: []
acceptance: `docs/decisions/0011-<slug>.md`, accepted, answering the question [0002](decisions/0002-control-and-bulk-separation.md) §7 left open and B-017 ran into: **a publisher writes fan-out on the connection the SUBSCRIBE arrived on, and nothing on the wire lets it choose a different one of that peer's per-path connections.** Until that is decided the control tier cannot carry SUBSCRIBE, and therefore cannot be justified at all (PROTOCOL §2.5). The options, each with its cost: (a) a HELLO key naming the dialled path, which makes the tiers distinguishable on the wire and contradicts §2.5's "nothing distinguishes them"; (b) SUBSCRIBE keeps riding its path's connection, which is today's behaviour and leaves an endpoint that publishes *and* subscribes on one path able to queue its own SUBSCRIBE behind its own payload; (c) fan-out rides the control connection, which puts payload on the tier meant to be free of it; (d) the fingerprint alone, which is ambiguous once a peer holds several per-path connections. Evidence to weigh: Zenoh's ALPN traffic classes, MOQT's control stream pair and iroh's one-connection-per-ALPN [prior-art §1, §2, §4], and the rule that a subscription is addressed to a *path* while HELLO and credit are addressed to a *peer*. SYNTHESIS §8.2's closing line is amended if the answer changes what 0002 decided.
note: the question turned out to be two questions wearing one coat, which is why 0002 §7 and 0008 §4.2 both thought they had answered it. 0008 answered *which connections are one peer* — the proved fingerprint, nothing else. What nobody had answered is *which connection a side writes its own traffic on*, and 0011 §4.1 decides it: **the one the registration arrived on.** Every protocol in the catalogue already does this and the sheets made it unanimous — EMQX sends a server-initiated PUBLISH "on the stream where it received that topic's subscription", a NATS `sid` is connection-local, a RabbitMQ consumer tag is channel-scoped, an AMQP 1.0 link is attached to one connection at a time and a second attach *steals* rather than addresses it. MQTT looks like the counter-example and is not: its session owns the association, and a session is precisely what weida does not have. The general rule that falls out is worth more than the answer: **a frame that names a path is path-scoped and rides that path's connection; a frame that names only the peer is peer-scoped.** Option E was the instructive rejection — naming another connection requires a stable identifier for it, which is a session identifier, excluded by PROTOCOL §11. **Consequence I did not expect when filing this:** the rule strikes the reserved L2 credit frame out of the control tier too (it names a subscription, which names a path), which amends 0003 §4.2 and leaves the tier with no cargo at all. So B-045 is parked, not merely unblocked, with a revival condition.
